  • Microsoft, OpenAI and Anthropic are investing millions to train teachers how to use AI Three AI companies are funding a $23 million academy with teachers’ unions to train 400,000 K-12 educators in using AI wisely and ethically.

  • AI Chatbots in K-12 Education: An Experimental Study of Socratic vs. Non-Socratic Approaches and the Role of Step-by-Step Reasoning In a randomized study of 14–16-year-olds, step-by-step AI explanations improved performance, while Socratic chatbots increased engagement without reliably improving learning.
  • Generative AI alone may not be enough: Evaluating AI Support for Learning Mathematical Proof An LLM tutor improved homework scores, but chatbots alone did not raise exam performance, suggesting AI tools need stronger learning-science design.
  • The Evidence Base on AI in K-12: A 2026 Review Stanford SCALE’s review finds causal research on K-12 AI is still thin, with short-term gains during AI use and mixed results when students work independently.
  • Effects of LLM Use and Note-Taking on Reading Comprehension and Memory: A Randomised Experiment in Secondary Schools Among 14–15-year-olds in England, note-taking—alone or with an LLM—beat using an LLM alone for comprehension and retention three days later.
  • MOOCs 2012-2022: An overview This review traces a decade of massive open online courses, highlighting their flexibility as well as persistent dropout and engagement problems.
  • The Evidence Base on AI in K-12: A November 2026 Review An updated Stanford review finds a growing but still limited causal evidence base, with AI more helpful when it supports learning rather than replacing student work.
  • Artificial intelligence in education: Addressing ethical challenges in K-12 settings This paper maps K-12 AI uses and ethical risks and points teachers to classroom resources for teaching AI ethics.
  • An Important Consideration Regarding AI and Education This legal analysis argues that putting tools like ChatGPT in schools can conflict with FERPA unless districts carefully check how student data is used.
  • Permission Slips to Use ChatGPT? Some Schools Say They’re Necessary Privacy experts say schools may need parental permission before students use ChatGPT, because student prompts can become training data.
